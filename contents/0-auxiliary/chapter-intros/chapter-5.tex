With the recent developments in quantum computing, there is a growing concern that assumptions such as the discrete logarithm or $q$-Diffie--Hellman, on which many previously considered systems such as Groth16 and PlonK rely, might be broken once a cryptographically relevant quantum computer (CRQC) is built.
This raises the question: how can we build $\Sigma$-protocols, or more general SNARKs, from assumptions that remain secure against a CRQC?
Such assumptions are called \emph{post-quantum assumptions}.

This part introduces two families of post-quantum zero-knowledge (PQZK) protocols:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Lattice-based.} These protocols are based on the hardness of certain problems over so-called \emph{lattices}, such as finding short vectors in a ``grid'' in a high-dimensional space.
    They resemble the classical elliptic curve-based approaches the most; for instance, one can directly rewrite $\Sigma$-protocols (see \Cref{section:lattice-sigma-protocols}) using the lattice-based machinery.
    Assumptions such as SIS and LWE are considered well-studied and conservative, while lattice-based proof systems such as LaBRADOR are among the most efficient post-quantum constructions.
    \item \textbf{Code-based.} These protocols combine error-correcting codes with hash functions, and their security relies only on arguably the most conservative assumptions, such as collision resistance of hash functions.
    The primary construction we consider is \emph{ZK-STARK}.
    To build it, we introduce error-correcting codes, Interactive Oracle Proofs of Proximity, the Fast Reed--Solomon Interactive Oracle Proof of Proximity (FRI), and the Algebraic Intermediate Representation (AIR).
\end{enumerate}
