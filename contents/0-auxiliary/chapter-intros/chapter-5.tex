With the recent developments in quantum computing, there is a large concern that assumptions such as discrete logarithm or $q$-Diffie-Hellman (which many previously considered systems such as Groth16 or PlonK rely on) might be broken with the introduction of cryptographically relevant quantum computer (CRQC).
Consequently, this raises the question: how can we build $\Sigma$-protocols or more general SNARK systems using cryptographic assumptions resistant against CRQC?
Additionally, we need to introduce exactly such type of assumptions called \emph{post-quantum assumptions}.

This section introduces two families of post-quantum zero-knowledge (PQZK) protocols:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Lattice-based.} Such protocols are based on difficulty of solving certain problems over so-called \emph{lattices}, such as finding short vectors lying on a ``grid'' in a high-dimensional space.
    Such protocols resemble the classical elliptic curve-based approaches most; for instance, one can directly rewrite $\Sigma$-protocols (see \Cref{section:lattice-sigma-protocols}) using the lattice-based machinery.
    Assumptions such as SIS and LWE are considered highly secure and conservative while folding schemes on lattices are more efficient than many other PQ constructions.
    \item \textbf{Hash-based.} These protocols are based on arguably the most conservative assumptions such as hash preimage resistance.
    The primary construction we are going to consider is \emph{ZK-STARK}.
    We introduce the notions of error-correcting codes, fast Reed-Solomon Interactive Oracle Proof of Proximity (FRI), and Algebraic Intermediate Representation (AIR).
\end{enumerate}
